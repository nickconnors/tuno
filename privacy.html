Tuno Support Privacy

Privacy

Tuno: IPTV Player. Last updated 13 November 2026.

Tuno collects nothing. It has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. What you add to Tuno stays on your device.

What stays on your device

  • Your playlists: the server address, username and password, or the M3U link. Passwords and links are kept in the device's Keychain.
  • The channels, TV guide, movies and shows loaded from your provider.
  • Your favorites, My List, what you watched and where you stopped, hidden categories and settings.
  • Your downloads.

None of this is sent to the developer. The developer runs no server that receives anything from Tuno, and cannot see what you add or watch.

Who Tuno connects to

  • Your provider. Tuno connects to the server in your playlist to load its lists, guide, pictures and video, and sends that server your login for it, because that is how the provider knows you. A playlist can also point to other servers the provider chose, for pictures or the TV guide; Tuno fetches those as the playlist gives them. Like any website you visit, those servers see your IP address. How they handle it is up to them.
  • Guide links you add yourself in Settings.
  • This website, for the demo only. If you enter demo as the server, Tuno streams and downloads one sample video from this site. The site is hosted by GitHub Pages, and GitHub may log the IP addresses of requests (GitHub's privacy statement). Nothing about you is sent. Without a connection the demo plays the same video from inside the app.

Apple

If you chose to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may give the developer anonymous crash reports and usage numbers. That is Apple's feature and follows Apple's privacy policy; Tuno adds nothing to it. Backups of your device include Tuno's data like any other app's (downloads are left out).

Children

Tuno is not directed at children, and collects nothing from anyone.
